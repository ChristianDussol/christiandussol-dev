# Ship to Production, Season 4

**Technical review panel.** CloudBro, Seoul. Season 4 of the Ship to Production open source incubation programme. October 2026 to January 2027.

Ship to Production is CloudBro's open source incubation programme: twelve weeks spent taking early projects from something that works for the person who wrote it to something another team can run. Season 4 opened with six projects and thirteen builders, with Upstage providing the models and Zadara the infrastructure.

I joined the technical review panel for this season, alongside Sanghyun Park and Roberto T.

## What the work involves

Reviewing the six teams at checkpoints across the twelve weeks. The first session is on 31 October in Seoul, which is also the day season 3 presents its final results.

A review panel is not there to ask the questions a demo answers. Does this run anywhere other than the machine it was built on. What happens when the dependency it assumes is missing. Who operates it once the programme ends. Those are the questions that decide whether an open source project survives its first season, and they are rarely the ones a builder has had time to ask.

## What the teams are building

Six problems, and three of them sit on ground this site already covers. One team is building an agent governance platform: an agent registry, permission management and integrated audit, which is the question I have been circling all year with MCP. Another is building a self-improvement engine for agents that connects scattered knowledge to working memory, so that only repeatedly verified experience accumulates. A third is building a production-grade Kubernetes operator for CUBRID, covering failover, backup and restore.

The other three: an evaluation and observability platform that ties LLM and agent execution to quality and cost, a failure diagnosis tool that matches live environments and logs against known issues, and a semantic drift detector for enterprise agent ontologies.
